# Guide 01: Rotary cutting + first two hand stitches

**Time:** ~3 h · **Sampler cards:** #1 running stitch, #2 backstitch
**You need:** mat, rotary cutter, 6×24 ruler, Kai shears, chalk pen, washed muslin, hand needles, scarlet thread, pins

| Block | Min |
|---|---|
| 0. Setup | 10 |
| 1. Rotary-cut squares | 45 |
| Break | 10 |
| 2. Thread + knot | 15 |
| 3. Card #1 running stitch | 35 |
| 4. Card #2 backstitch | 45 |
| 5. Label + photo | 10 |

---

## 0. Setup (10 min)
1. Clear the whole table. No drinks on it.
2. Mat on the right half, centered over the fold seam.
3. Lamp aimed at where your hands will be.
4. Lay out the tools in reach. Put the cutter on the side of your cutting hand, **blade closed**.

## 1. Rotary-cut 8"×8" squares (45 min)

**Words you need**
- **Selvage:** the two finished long edges of the fabric. They don't fray and are woven tighter (often slightly different texture, sometimes tiny holes).
- **Grain:** the threads run parallel to the selvage. Cutting square to the grain keeps the squares from twisting when sewn.

**Safety, non-negotiable**
- Close the blade **every** time you set the cutter down.
- Fingers spread flat on the ruler, at least 1/2" from the cutting edge.
- Cut **away** from your body. Standing gives you more pressure and control.

**Steps**
1. **Rough-cut a working piece with the shears**, about 18" × 34", with one long edge being the selvage. (Your muslin is 63" wide, wider than the 36" mat. Working in mat-sized pieces avoids folding, and folding skews the cuts.)
2. **Lay it on the mat** single layer, selvage at the bottom, running along a horizontal grid line. Smooth it with your hands. Small wrinkles are OK; big ones, pull flat.
3. **Trim off the selvage.** Lay the ruler horizontally so its edge is ~1/2" above the selvage. Press down on the ruler, then cut along the ruler edge in one smooth push. Remove the strip. *Why: selvage is woven tighter and shrinks/stretches differently from the rest.*
4. **Square the left end.** Lay the ruler vertically near the left end. Line up one of the ruler's horizontal lines exactly with your clean bottom edge. Cut along the ruler. Now you have a true 90° corner.
5. **Cut an 8" strip.** Your ruler is only 6" wide, so use the mat grid: put the clean bottom edge on a mat line, lay the ruler on the mat line 8" above it, and check that both ends sit on that line. Cut.
6. **Cut the strip into squares.** Turn the strip so its squared end is on a vertical mat line. Cut at 8", 16", 24", 32". That's 4 squares.
7. **Repeat** steps 5–6 for a second strip, then rough-cut another piece if needed. **Target: 12 squares.** More is fine.
8. **Check:** measure a few. Within 1/8" of 8" is good. If one is way off, keep it as scrap; don't use it as a card.

**If it goes wrong**
- Fabric shifts under the ruler → press harder on the ruler and cut slower. Standing helps.
- Blade skips threads → you lifted pressure mid-cut. Re-cut along the same line.
- Ruler slides → hold it with your fingertips, not your palm.

## 2. Thread the needle + quilter's knot (15 min)
1. **Cut ~18" of scarlet thread** (about elbow to fingertip, plus a bit). Longer thread tangles and frays from being pulled through so many times.
2. **Cut the end at an angle** with the shears. A clean, angled tip goes through the eye easily. Don't lick it; that just makes it limp.
3. **Thread it through the eye**, pull about 4" through. This is **single thread**: you sew with the long end, the short tail just rides along.
4. **Quilter's knot** (at the end of the long tail):
   1. Hold the needle pointing up. Lay the end of the long thread along the needle, with the end slightly past the tip.
   2. Wrap the thread around the needle **3 times**, near the tip.
   3. Pinch the wraps between thumb and finger.
   4. With the wraps still pinched, pull the needle up through them, all the way down the thread, until the knot forms at the end.
   5. Trim any tail past the knot to ~1/8".
5. Practice the knot 3 times before you start. A neat knot is a small, firm ball.

## 3. Card #1: running stitch (35 min)
**What it's for:** basting, which means temporary stitches that hold pleats or layers before machine-sewing. You'll use it on every skirt pleat.

1. **Mark three lines** with the chalk pen and ruler, 1" apart, starting 1" from one edge.
2. **Start from the back** so the knot is hidden: push the needle up from underneath at the left end of line 1.
3. **Row 1 (big stitches, ~1/2"):** go down 1/2" along the line, come up 1/2" further. Stitch and gap are equal.
   ```
   front:  ——   ——   ——   ——
   back:      ——   ——   ——
   ```
4. **Loading:** once it feels natural, rock the needle in and out 3–4 times *before* pulling the thread through. This is faster and straighter.
5. **Tie off** at the end of the row, on the back: make a tiny stitch, and before pulling it tight, pass the needle through the loop. Pull snug. Do this twice. Trim, leaving a ~1/4" tail.
6. **Row 2 at ~1/4", Row 3 at ~1/8".** Smaller is harder. That's the point.

**Check:** the stitches follow the chalk line, and the stitches in each row are about the same length.

## 4. Card #2: backstitch (45 min)
**What it's for:** the strongest hand stitch. Repairs, places the machine can't reach, securing ends. **This is your week 1 gate.**

1. New square, three chalk lines as before.
2. Knot, come up from the back **one stitch-length (1/8") in from the left end** of the line. Call this point A.
3. **Go back** and down at the very start of the line. (You've sewn backwards, which is the name.)
4. Come up **1/8" ahead of A**.
5. Go back down **into A**, the end of the previous stitch.
6. Repeat: *up one stitch ahead, back down into the end of the last stitch.*
   ```
   front:  ——————————————   (continuous, like a machine)
   back:   overlapping stitches, each twice as long
   ```
7. Tie off as before.
8. Row 1 at 1/4" to get the rhythm, then rows 2–3 at **1/8"**.

**Gate:** 10 stitches in a row at about 1/8", evenly sized, on the line. Nobody should be able to point to the odd one out.

**If it goes wrong**
- Thread twists and knots itself → every so often, let the needle dangle and spin free.
- Fabric puckers along the line → you're pulling too tight. Pull just until the thread lies flat.
- Gaps between stitches on the front → you're not going back down exactly into the end of the last stitch.
